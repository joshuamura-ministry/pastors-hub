# Terrain — the Sabbath guideline (the pastor's words, 1 Oct 2026)

> Some things can still be done on Sabbath; we are not legalistic. Preparing a meal doesn't have to wait till the sun
> goes down — our church is not like that. We can prepare meals and serve meals during the Sabbath day. There are things
> we can still do as long as it's serving, as long as it's meeting a need. If there's money being exchanged, or anything
> like that… if there's serious labour, even then that should be OK as long as it's something that is blessing people and
> helping people. So there has to be a guideline that gives a little more freedom. Obviously we're not going to be
> having a fair on the Sabbath, or balloons and raffles. We're going to be doing the things that Christ would do:
> healing ministry, seminars, feeding the homeless — all those things can be done on Sabbath. Have a better filter, so it
> understands that it's not legalistic here.

## The rule Terrain uses ("Fits the Sabbath")
The question is: **is it doing good — the things Christ did on the Sabbath?** (Matthew 12:12, "it is lawful to do well on
the sabbath days"; Mark 2:27; Luke 13:16; Luke 14:5.) If it blesses, heals, feeds, teaches, comforts, visits, welcomes
or serves people, it FITS the Sabbath — even when it takes real work.

**Fits the Sabbath (yes):**
- Worship, prayer, Bible study, seminars and evangelistic meetings, Sabbath School, music in worship.
- Healing and health ministry: health screenings, health talks, visiting the sick, hospital and nursing-home visits,
  anointing, mental-health support groups.
- Feeding people: preparing and serving meals, potlucks, fellowship lunches, feeding the homeless, delivering meals,
  a soup kitchen or a pantry giving food free.
- Mercy and practical help for someone in need: help after a disaster, caring for children so a worn-out parent can
  rest, helping a family in an emergency, even when it takes real labour ("the ox in the ditch").
- Visiting, welcoming, hospitality, friendship; nature walks and creation activities; Pathfinder and Adventurer
  activities that teach, serve or worship; youth programmes with a spiritual purpose; community service done free.

**Does not fit the Sabbath (no):**
- Buying and selling, prices, fees, fundraising, offerings for a project, sales, markets, auctions.
- Fairs, festivals with game booths, carnival-style events, balloons, parties for entertainment.
- Secular entertainment and competitive sports, sports tournaments.
- Ordinary business and routine work that is not meeting a present need (routine maintenance, renovation projects,
  administration and committee business, printing and mailing runs, set-up that could be done before).

**Drawings and giveaways (the pastor, 1 Oct 2026):** "Raffles and prizes are OK, because we do that sometimes for
Bibles during seminars. We have community giveaways that are part of our services." So FREE drawings and giveaways —
nothing bought, nothing paid to enter (a Bible drawn among the guests at a seminar, a gift basket at a health fair, a
community giveaway) — are fine, on the Sabbath too when they are part of the meeting or the service. What stays out is a
raffle that SELLS tickets or chances (gambling or fundraising by chance), on any day. A connection card may offer a free
drawing, but the drawing never requires anything beyond what the person chooses to give.

**His calls on the borderline groups (1 Oct 2026):** meeting at a café or diner where food or drink is bought: best on
another day (it fits when the idea names a home, church, library or park). Planned practical help that is not an emergency
(car-care day, fix-it day, building a ramp): best on another day; a real emergency still fits. The back-to-school backpack
giveaway: fits the Sabbath as a free giveaway (keep the games for another day).

**When in doubt:** if it is mercy or meeting a need, it fits; if it is commerce, entertainment or routine work, choose
another day. Terrain offers Sabbath hours only for ideas that fit, and always lets the pastor decide for his church.
